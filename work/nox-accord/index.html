NOX ACCORD

A research build exploring whether independent parties can coordinate over private economic inputs without revealing every input or handing the full decision to a trusted intermediary.

NOX ACCORD is intentionally distinct from Concord. Its focus is not a persistent syndicated-capital relationship; it is the narrower coordination problem of private offer submission, confidential comparison, bounded allocation and selective disclosure before a public consequence.

01 / Problem

Coordination often asks parties to reveal more than the next decision actually requires.

The experiment starts from a simple asymmetry: a joint outcome may require comparing private inputs without requiring those inputs to become shared public state.

NOX ACCORD asks whether the comparison and allocation step can remain confidential while the resulting authority and public consequence stay bounded and inspectable.

02 / Model

Privacy is treated as selective visibility, not an all-or-nothing property.

The design question is not “hide everything.” It is which information must become visible at each boundary for the next actor to act safely.

Private input

An economic term or constraint that should not automatically become shared state.

Comparison

The confidential step that evaluates compatible or preferable inputs.

Bounded allocation

The selected outcome is constrained rather than granting unrestricted authority.

Selective disclosure

Only the result required for the next step is revealed.

Public consequence

The externally actionable result exists separately from the private formation process.

03 / What I built

The system was built as an experimental apparatus for confidential procurement and allocation semantics.

Its contribution to the portfolio is the separation between private formation and public consequence, and the explicit treatment of selection, allocation and disclosure as different stages rather than one opaque “private market” operation.

Offer submission

Private economic inputs enter the coordination process without becoming public by default.

Selection logic

Comparison is treated independently from final execution.

Allocation semantics

The selected outcome remains bounded by the decision context.

Disclosure boundary

The system exposes only what the next action requires rather than the complete private state.

04 / Technical decision

Confidentiality is useful only if authority remains legible at the point of consequence.

A private comparison should not silently become unrestricted execution authority.

This distinction later became important in other systems: private coordination may produce a public commitment, but it should not erase who can act, what was selected or which authority boundary remains in force.

06 / Boundary

The project should be read as an experiment, not a production market.

NOX ACCORD does not claim audited financial security, a production confidential exchange, or universal confidentiality guarantees. Its value is the coordination model it tested and the privacy/authority boundaries that later reappear in other projects.

This is also why it remains separate from Concord: similar privacy questions, different economic object and different lifecycle.

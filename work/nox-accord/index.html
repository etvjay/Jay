NOX ACCORD

A confidentiality experiment around private economic inputs, comparison, bounded allocation, and selective disclosure.

Research questionPositionExperimentBuildEvidenceBoundaryLineage

Research question

Can independent parties coordinate over private economic inputs without revealing the inputs themselves or delegating the entire decision to a trusted intermediary?

Position

Confidentiality matters when it expands the set of interactions parties can safely coordinate. The system should reveal only the outcome required for the next step while preserving bounded authority around selection and allocation.

Experiment

Explore private offer submission, confidential comparison, conditional selection, bounded allocation, selective decryption, and a public lifecycle around the final executable result.

Build

Private inputs → confidential comparison → bounded allocation → selective disclosure → public consequence.

NOX ACCORD is treated here as a distinct research build from Concord. It investigates confidential procurement/allocation semantics rather than persistent syndicated-capital relationships.

Evidence

  • Confidential-input coordination model implemented as a separate experimental system.
  • Selection and allocation modeled independently from public settlement.
  • Selective-disclosure boundary used to avoid treating privacy as an all-or-nothing property.

Current boundary

NOX ACCORD is presented as a research build, not as a production confidential market or audited financial protocol. Its value in the portfolio is the question it tested and the design primitives later explored elsewhere.

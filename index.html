This archive is organized around technical questions and experiments rather than products. The code is evidence for an argument; failed hypotheses remain part of the record.

Featured research 01 / OpenRails

2D Nonce Lanes

What changes when replay protection is scoped to an account and an execution lane instead of one account-wide sequence?

A code-first investigation into Ethereum’s sequential account nonce, ERC-4337 keyed nonces, and OpenRails’ application-level account × channel → sequence model. The question is not whether one model is superior, but where independent ordering domains are useful.

Featured research 02 / RailsCard

Dynamic Authorization Lanes

When a nonce lane stops meaning “execution queue” and starts identifying one independently exercisable authorization.

Deferred RailsCards exposed a specific failure: two valid pre-signed authorizations could invalidate one another when they shared a sequential lane. The resulting design allocates a fresh replay domain to each outstanding authorization.

About

The unit of work is a question that survives implementation.

I use working systems to test claims about coordination, authority, evidence and autonomy. A result belongs here when the mechanism is inspectable and the boundary of the claim is explicit.

Research stories compare the mechanism against its actual substrate and established alternatives. A difference is not automatically an improvement; the point is to identify the conditions under which the construction is useful and what it costs.

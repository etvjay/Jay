01 / Pressure

A payment does not always need to execute when it is authorized.

Some economic actions are naturally deferred: a card can be issued now, shared or stored, and exercised later by an intended recipient or eligible bearer.

authorize now
   ↓
portable / deferred instrument
   ↓
claim later
   ↓
funding succeeds
   ↓
Paycard state exists
   ↓
settlement

The design problem is to preserve exact payment terms and replay safety across that delay without confusing “the payer signed this” with “the payer has already transferred funds.”

02 / Existing EVM substrate

Allowance, permit and payment authorization solve different problems.

ERC-20 approve lets a spender withdraw tokens up to an allowance. ERC-2612 permit lets the owner modify that allowance through an EIP-712 signature instead of an onchain approve transaction.

RailsCard is not another token allowance standard. Its signed object carries payment-specific semantics—recipient mode, allocation, flow velocity, start, lifespan, recovery destination and Hub replay coordinates—and is later consumed by the OpenRails settlement contract.

ERC-20 allowance

How much may this spender withdraw from this token owner?

ERC-2612 permit

Can the owner update that allowance by signature?

RailsCard

What exact deferred payment authority did this payer issue, and under what claim/settlement terms?

Paycard state

What funded economic state actually exists after the claim succeeds?

03 / Construction

The interface distinguishes bearer and recipient-bound authority.

type RailsCard = {
  variant: "bearer" | "recipient-bound";
  claimPolicy?: "first-claimant" | "recipient-only";

  paymentTerms: PaymentTerms;
  proofPolicy: ProofPolicy;

  intentRef?: IntentRef;
  workspaceRef?: WorkspaceRef;
  pathRef?: PathRef;
  proposalRef?: ProposalRef;
  pactRef?: PactRef;

  settlementIntentRef?: SettlementIntentRef;
  paycardRef?: PaycardStreamRef;

  status:
    | "DRAFT"
    | "PREPARED"
    | "AWAITING_AUTHORIZATION"
    | "COMMITTED"
    | "EXECUTING"
    | "SETTLED"
    | "FAILED"
    | "CANCELLED"
    | "EXPIRED";

  signatureBinding: SignatureBinding;
};

A bearer card can sign a wildcard recipient and bind the actual claimant at claim time. A recipient-bound card commits to the intended recipient before the card is shared.

04 / Claim path

The Hub verifies the signed economic terms before creating funded state.

bytes32 structHash = keccak256(abi.encode(
  ENVELOPE_TYPEHASH,
  paycardId,
  metadataHash,
  signedRecipient,
  totalAllocationPool,
  flowVelocityPerSecond,
  genesisTimestamp,
  lifespanSeconds,
  residualDeltaRecipient,
  nonceChannel,
  nonceValue
));

payer = recoverSigner(
  keccak256("\x19\x01" || DOMAIN_SEPARATOR || structHash),
  envelopeSignature
);

Only after signature recovery and replay consumption does the Hub attempt to pull funds:

_consumeNonce(payer, nonceChannel, nonceValue);
_safeTransferFrom(
  arcUsdc,
  payer,
  address(this),
  totalAllocationPool
);

registry[paycardId] = PaycardRegistry(...);

For bearer cards, the signed recipient can be zero while the claimant is stored as the eventual recipient. The payer therefore authorizes the bearer form deliberately rather than the contract silently dropping a recipient restriction.

05 / Authorization ≠ funding

A valid RailsCard can still fail to become funded state.

The signature proves authorization of the signed terms. It does not prove future balance, allowance, token behavior, or successful escrow at claim time.

valid signature
   ≠
funds already escrowed

claim success requires:
  valid signature
  + unused replay coordinate
  + valid time/state
  + successful token transferFrom

This distinction is important enough to preserve in product status. A deferred authorization should not be presented as financially completed merely because the signature exists.

06 / Replay domains

Deferral exposed a concurrency problem.

When several RailsCards were signed before any were claimed, a shared sequential Hub nonce made those otherwise independent authorizations compete for the same replay slot.

// old pressure
A -> channel 0 / nonce 12
B -> channel 0 / nonce 12

claim(B) -> nonce 13
claim(A) -> invalid

The follow-up Dynamic Authorization Lanes experiment allocates a fresh non-zero lane to each deferred card while reserving channel zero for immediate sequential opens.

07 / Boundary

RailsCard is a signed deferred payment authorization.

It is not a token standard, not a prepaid balance by definition, and not evidence that escrow has already occurred.

Its usefulness comes from composing existing EVM mechanisms—typed signatures, contract-level replay protection and token transfer—with OpenRails-specific payment and relationship semantics.

signed authority
≠ allowance
≠ funded escrow
≠ final settlement

Variants

  • recipient-bound
  • bearer / first claimant
  • one-time terms
  • streaming terms

Cryptographic boundary

The EIP-712 signature binds the payment terms. Replay protection and funding remain application/contract responsibilities.

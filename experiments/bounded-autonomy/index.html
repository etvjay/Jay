Bounded Autonomy

What is the smallest authority an autonomous actor needs to complete an economic objective?

HypothesisApparatusVariablesEvidenceCurrent resultFalsification
01 / Hypothesis

Useful autonomy does not require unrestricted possession of the underlying authority.

An autonomous system can act continuously if authority is scoped by capability, amount, time, resource, dependency and revocation rather than by full wallet custody.

02 / Systems under test
OpenRails ↗

Tests persistent bounded delegation through Paths and deferred authority through RailsCards.

OTC Chronograph ↗

Tests conditional capital authority where action depends on DAG readiness and failure mode.

03 / Variables
capability scope
amount ceiling
frequency / period
velocity / lifespan

04 / Evidence

OpenRails has runtime-enforced Path limits and testnet evidence for allowed versus over-limit proposals. Chronograph contributes explicit dependency masks and branch-local failure behavior.

05 / Current result

Authority can persist longer than one action while remaining narrower than ownership.

The recurring design pressure is therefore not “human or autonomous,” but how much authority should survive between actions and under which conditions.

06 / What would falsify it

If repeated autonomous execution cannot be made reliable without unrestricted custody or constant human re-authorization, the bounded-authority model fails for those workflows.
